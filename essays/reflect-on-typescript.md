When I first started as a freshman at UH Manoa, my introduction to coding was all Java and a little bit of C++. In those classes, setting variable types and dealing with strict compilers was just standard practice. Fast forward to this semester in ICS 314 as a junior, and we were introduced to JavaScript. We spent some time doing basic exercises, and honestly, I had a blast with it. JavaScript felt smooth, easy to work with, and fast to run. But right after getting comfortable with it, we switched straight over to TypeScript the very next day.

<div class="clearfix"></div>

## Initial Frustration with the Switch

To be completely honest, I didn't like TypeScript at first. It felt annoying to spend time learning JavaScript only to immediately jump into something else that had a bunch of different rules. Having the compiler constantly throw errors at me after experiencing the freedom of JavaScript made it feel like I was taking two steps backward. I kept asking myself why we were adding all this extra complexity when JavaScript was working just fine.

## How TypeScript Grew on Me

It’s been about three weeks now, and TypeScript has actually grown on me quite a bit. Going through the continuous practice exercises made me realize how it connects back to what I learned early on in Java. TypeScript basically takes the flexibility of JavaScript and adds back the strict type safety system I was already used to from my freshman year. Catching dumb errors or missing properties right in the text editor saves a ton of pain compared to hunting down bugs when running the code.

## Tackling Timed WODs

The practice WODs (Workouts of the Day) under tight time limits haven't been easy, but they definitely forced me to get better. 

* **First Attempts:** My first try on a new WOD is usually pretty slow. I end up wasting time fighting compiler errors and double-checking syntax.
* **Repetition:** By attempt 3 to 5, my times get much closer to average. Repeating the problems forces the syntax into my head so I don't have to keep looking up documentation.

I’m definitely not the fastest or best coder in class, and my timing still needs work, but I can tell I understand the material way better now than I did three weeks ago.

## Wrapping Up

Looking back, I get why our professor threw us into TypeScript so quickly. It gives you the structure you actually need if you want to build clean, reliable web apps. The timed WODs can be stressful, but the repeated practice helped me get past my initial frustration and turned TypeScript into something I actually appreciate using.
