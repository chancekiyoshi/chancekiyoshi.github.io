To fix slow internet, physical line drops and bad connection speeds across administrative offices and classrooms, the COM-FSM IT team replaced legacy cabling infrastructure with fresh CAT5 ethernet runs. The goal was to establish uniform cabling standards across all facilities to ensure gigabit connectivity and cleaner patch panel routing.

I was responsible for tracing old lines, pulling new twisted-pair cables through walls and ceiling drops, terminating cables into RJ-45 keystone jacks, and organizing server rack patch panels. It was tough because many lines were outdated. I assisted my supervisor in systematic line continuity testing across hundreds of node drops to verify pinout configurations and ensure signal quality before final sign-off.

This hands-on work sharpened my understanding of OSI Layer 1 concepts, structured cabling standards, and clean cable management practices necessary for maintainable, enterprise-level network environments.
